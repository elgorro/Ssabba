# The experience

The [Concept]({{< relref "../concept" >}}) describes what Ssabba stores and who owns it. This
section describes what it is like to use — the surfaces a member meets, what each is for, and the
things Ssabba deliberately does not do.

The shape of a week in a club is small and repetitive: someone proposes a date, people say whether
they can make it, a few turn up, sets get played, somebody writes down the scores, and the ladder
moves. Everything here hangs off that loop. Nothing here is a destination in its own right.

## The surfaces

| Surface | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **The session** | The centre of gravity. Who is coming, who is on the waiting list, which court, what it costs, what the weather did. Most of a member's time is spent here. |
| **The match** | A result: sets, teams, the rating each player carried in and out. Read after the fact, entered quickly during. |
| **The ladder** | Standing within *this* community. Never a global ranking. |
| **The person** | A profile: what they play, how long they have played, the badges their community gave them — and, if they opted in, that they are looking for someone to play with. |
| **The community** | Its rules, its seasons, its venues, its members, its books. |
| **Discovery** | The only surface that looks outward: open sessions and people looking to play, filtered by when, where and roughly how well. |

## What Ssabba is not

Ssabba borrows some vocabulary from social software — reactions, sharing, badges — and it is worth
being plain about how far that goes, because the words carry expectations the product does not meet.

- **There is no feed.** No timeline, no "what you missed", no ordering of anything by popularity.
  A reaction is read on the thing it is attached to, and nowhere else.
- **There is no follower graph.** You are a member of communities. You do not follow people, and
  nobody accumulates an audience.
- **There are no vanity metrics.** No like counts on a profile, no streaks, no engagement score.
  `ReliabilityScore` exists, but it measures whether you turn up when you said you would, which is a
  fact about a club and not a game.
- **There is no messaging.** Ssabba is not where the group chats; every group already has somewhere
  for that.
- **Badges are not points.** A community defines its own and awards them by hand. They do not add
  up to anything and they do not travel.
